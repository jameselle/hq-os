# Trend Radar playbook: find and use video ideas

Use this from **Market & Competitors → Trend Radar** after choosing the business you are researching. Keep each business's accounts and findings in its own HQ workspace. You can use the three routes independently; start with competitors when you want a repeatable watchlist, or with Feed discoveries when you want ideas from your own recommendations.

## First: watch competitors and their strongest posts

1. Open **Manage niches** and choose the closest research category. Save it. If no preset fits, add a niche with a specific search phrase.
2. Open **Find competitors**. Paste your Instagram or TikTok profile link, choose the platform and niche, and click **Find competitors**. Review each suggestion's reason and source. Select relevant accounts, then click **Watch selected**. You can also add a known handle under **Watched accounts**. Saving an account in HQ does not follow it on Instagram or TikTok.
3. Click **Scan now**. In **Top competitor posts**, filter to your niche and platform and choose **Most views**. Open the original posts. Record the opening line, visual hook, format and reason you think the post held attention. Choose **Fastest current growth** to look for current momentum after HQ has measured the same post more than once.
4. For ongoing research, enable **Automatic competitor discovery** for the seed if you want HQ to refresh suggestions daily or weekly. Choose whether HQ only suggests accounts or adds eligible direct competitors automatically. Review the watchlist and source health regularly.

## Second: look for small-creator breakouts

Open **Small-account breakouts**. Set a maximum follower count, minimum views and minimum views per follower. Open qualifying posts and compare their format with the creator's usual content. If the list is empty, add relevant smaller accounts to the watchlist, scan them, or adjust the thresholds. This section only covers watched Instagram and TikTok accounts with readable, recent public follower and view counts.

## Third: collect your own Explore or For You feed

1. Open **Feed discoveries**. Choose Instagram or TikTok, the research niche, a time limit and a post limit; then click **Create feed session**.
2. Install the Chrome extension once: click **Download Chrome extension** in HQ, open the downloaded ZIP to extract its folder, then open `chrome://extensions` in Chrome. Turn on **Developer mode**, click **Load unpacked**, and select the extracted folder containing `manifest.json`. Keep that folder in place. After an update, click **Reload** for the extension in Chrome.
3. Open **Instagram Explore** from HQ or your TikTok For You feed in Chrome. Click the extension icon, choose the waiting HQ session for the intended business and niche, and press **Start**. There is no pairing code. Keep that feed tab visible until the post or time limit stops the session.
4. Back in HQ, click **Check public views**. Open the saved posts, confirm the creator and public evidence, and use **Watch this creator** when the account belongs in your ongoing research. A saved Explore tile can initially be only a link; an unavailable counter remains unavailable.

## Turn research into something to film

For each useful post, keep the original link and note **what happened**, **the hook or structure**, **the public evidence and its measurement time**, and **your own version for this business**. Shortlist three ideas, choose one you can film with your own product and story, and check later scans to see whether the measured growth continued. A high lifetime view count, one personalised feed sample, or a single scan cannot establish that a format is currently spreading across a platform.

HQ targets a new post and view-count check about every 15 minutes while its collector and this Mac are running. Growth needs repeated observations at least 12 minutes apart; the first scan builds a baseline. If posts are missing, check the selected niche, watched accounts, platform filter, last scan time and source errors before drawing a conclusion.

For the signal definitions, collection limits and troubleshooting, see the [full Trend Radar guide](/guides/trend-radar).
